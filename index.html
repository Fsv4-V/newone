Project Brief

What Are We Building?

An intelligent wearable patch for diabetic patients with chronic foot ulcers that continuously tracks key wound biomarkers without removing the dressing.

The Clinical Problem

Chronic wounds including diabetic foot ulcers (DFUs) cost global healthcare systems over $30 billion annually. Current standard of care requires physical dressing removal for assessment — introducing infection risk, disrupting tissue, and causing patient pain.

Persistent alkaline wound pH (7.2–9.0), elevated temperature (>1.1°C differential), and biofilm impedance are key early indicators of infection that currently go undetected between clinic visits.

Our Solution

A hybrid-architecture smart patch: a low-cost disposable flexible PCB (FPCB) wound-contact layer paired with a reusable BLE microcontroller module — enabling 24/7 wound monitoring via smartphone.

Target Specifications

The patch must operate continuously within strict biocompatibility and safety constraints while remaining comfortable for diabetic patients during daily activities.

Project Timeline

W1
Literature Review: pH Variations & Wound Healing Biology
✓ Complete

The first week focused on establishing a solid foundation of the clinical and biochemical basis for the project. Extensive research was conducted on wound healing stages and how pH changes at each phase indicate healing status or infection.

  • Studied the four wound healing stages: Hemostasis, Inflammation (pH ~7.0), Proliferation (pH drops 4–6), and Maturation/Remodelling
  • Reviewed published literature on pH variations in chronic vs acute diabetic wounds — handwritten pH variation analysis notes compiled
  • Conducted comprehensive sensor comparison across 15 wound biomarkers: pH, temperature, moisture, impedance, VOC, blood perfusion, glucose, lactate, and more
  • Reviewed the clinical paper: pH variations in chronic diabetic wounds (s13047_023_00653) — a key foundational reference on infection biomarker thresholds
  • Reviewed: Advanced Transducer Architectures for Smart Wound Dressings — systematic review of ISFET, metal-oxide, and printed electrode technologies
  • Created Smart_Diabetic_Wound_Monitoring.xlsx — full project overview, parameter matrix (15 parameters), technology comparison, and commercial sensor shortlist

W2
Similar Products Analysis, Flexible PCB Research & Wearable Sensor Requirements
✓ Complete

Week two shifted to competitive analysis and engineering feasibility. Identified existing commercial and academic solutions, studied inkjet-printed flexible circuits, and extracted detailed circuit requirements from published wearable sensor reviews.

  • Identified SmartHEAL (James Dyson Award 2022, Warsaw University of Technology) as the closest comparable product — pH RFID sensor in wound dressing, battery-free, mass-producible at 0.5 PLN/sensor
  • Catalogued YouTube documentation and news references (Reuters, Euronews) on similar wound monitoring patches for competitive gap analysis
  • Studied: Flexible, Wearable and Fully-Printed Smart Patch for pH and Hydration Sensing in Wounds (Iversen et al., 2022) — extracted key design constraints: carbon paste electrodes, Ag/AgCl reference, −50 mV/pH sensitivity
  • Deep-dived into inkjet bioprinting paper (IJB-8-1-447): studied roll-to-roll FPCB manufacturing, conductive ink properties and layer thicknesses for printed sensors
  • Extracted circuit requirements from Wearable Sensors for Wound Healing-Related pH and Temperature Detection (Tang et al., 2021): high-Z op-amp buffers, ADC resolution, thresholds for healing vs infection
  • Reviewed wound healing pathology from Robins Basic Pathology — engineering analysis of wound healing dynamics

W3
pH Sensor Selection Matrix, Datasheet Analysis & Fabrication Research
✓ Complete

Week three was the most detailed engineering analysis week — systematic pH sensor comparison matrices, component datasheet deep-dives, and investigation of medical-grade fabrication partners.

  • Built iterative pH sensor selection matrix — compared Integrated ISFET (Clinical), Micro-Flex PCB (EEE Prototype), Printed Bio-Patch (Research), Optical+IR (Non-Invasive), and Academic SoC across 14 technical parameters
  • Final ranking: Micro-Flex PCB (JLCPCB) scored 9.5/10 for FYP feasibility — best balance of accuracy (±0.15 pH), cost ($40–80 parts), and buildability
  • Analysed: Shirzaei Sani et al. vs Iversen et al. — technical comparison of two state-of-the-art wearable wound patches to determine best design approaches to adopt
  • Analysed: Comparative matrix — Shirzaei Sani vs Tang et al. — electrode materials, sensing mechanisms, and clinical validation data
  • Deep-dived datasheets: AD5940/AD5941 (Analog Devices) potentiostat/EIS AFE — extracted key specs for pH and impedance measurement circuit design
  • Deep-dived: AD8603/AD8607/AD8609 (Analog Devices) — medical-grade precision op-amp for high-impedance pH buffer circuit
  • Researched TDS_Sensor_pH_303 ceramic reference electrode — evaluated for clinical-grade reference implementation
  • Identified medical-grade fabrication partners: Zimmer & Peacock (UK, printed electrodes), Microsens SA (Switzerland, ISFET chips), Sentron/Millar (Netherlands, bare die ISFET), Analog Devices Healthcare (USA, AD8603 + AD5940)
